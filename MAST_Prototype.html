The Two-Mechanism Model

Mechanism 1: Nutrient-Driven Permeability

Glutamine depletion triggers the CpxA/CpxR stress response, downregulating OmpF porins. This structural adaptation closes the primary channel for antibiotic influx (Vin), conferring functional resistance without genetic mutation.

Mechanism 2: Starvation-Induced Gene Dosage

Host starvation activates the ppGpp alarmone. This cascade derepresses silent resistance determinants (e.g., tetA, aadA) and drives plasmid copy number (PCN) amplification up to 89-fold, neutralizing internalized drugs.

Methodology & References

This prototype instantiates the three-module MAST architecture using HTML5, Tailwind CSS, and Chart.js. The mathematical logic is based on the following foundational literature:

  • [1] Ahmad, M., et al. (2025). The role of bacterial metabolism in antimicrobial resistance. Nature Reviews Microbiology.
  • [2] Belanger, C.R., Hancock, R.E.W. (2021). Testing physiologically relevant conditions in minimal inhibitory concentration assays. Nat Protoc, 16, 3761–3774.
  • [3] Batchelor E, Walthers D, Kenney LJ, Goulian M (2005). The Escherichia coli CpxA-CpxR Envelope Stress Response System Regulates Expression of the Porins OmpF and OmpC. J Bacteriol, 187.
  • [4] Wang, H., & Joffré, E. (2025). Plasmid copy number as a modulator in bacterial pathogenesis and antibiotic resistance. npj Antimicrobials and Resistance.
